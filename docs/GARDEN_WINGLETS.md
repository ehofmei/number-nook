# The Garden Winglets Collection

Garden Winglets is Number Nook's fourth ordinary collection and first bird collection. It is rooted in real North American garden and backyard birds so a companion can feel like someone the family might spot outdoors, while still fitting the game's warm storybook world.

Catalog 2.7 introduced all ten permanent entries, themes, personalities, signature dialogue, reusable CSS motifs, and Classic SVG portraits. Catalog 2.8 completes the collection with ten optimized Polished Sticker portraits and retains safe per-style fallback for compatibility.

## Bird-watching direction

These are recognizable species rather than vague bird-shaped fantasy characters. The family-requested anchors include an American goldfinch, blue jay, ruby-throated hummingbird, northern cardinal, white-breasted nuthatch, and red-bellied woodpecker. Four complementary species provide different proportions, behaviors, and field marks.

The art simplifies anatomy for friendliness, but each design should preserve the clues that make the species identifiable:

- use a bird's actual beak, crest, tail, wing, and body proportions as the starting silhouette;
- keep major field marks, such as the blue jay's necklace, nuthatch's black cap, woodpecker's barred back, and Carolina wren's white eyebrow;
- let small birds fill the portrait so they feel as substantial as cats and dogs in the same UI;
- show at least one clear wing or body edge instead of turning the design into a round face with feathers;
- use garden scenery and one restrained signature motif; do not obscure the bird with costumes or props;
- avoid cat-like muzzles, human hands, extra wings or feet, written text, and scientifically misleading features;
- favor friendly expressions and dynamic natural poses without making every bird smile in the same way.

## Classic art briefs

### Tuck — Common

A white-breasted nuthatch with a black cap and nape, white face and breast, blue-gray back, short tail, and pointed bill. Show the species' signature headfirst descent on a pale trunk rather than a generic branch perch.

### Marigold — Common

A vivid yellow American goldfinch with black forehead and wings, white wing bars, a compact seed-eating beak, and one orange marigold. Keep the Common design exceptionally cheerful and readable.

### Bluebell — Common

A lively blue jay with a swept crest, black necklace, white cheeks, barred blue wings, and a bluebell bloom. The crest and strong color blocks should carry the locked silhouette.

### Tumble — Common

A plump northern bobwhite with a short tail, striped cream-and-brown face, mottled warm body, and three broad stepping stones. Do not add a decorative head plume; northern bobwhites rely on facial stripes and round ground-bird proportions.

### Zinnia — Uncommon

A tiny green ruby-throated hummingbird hovering beside a coral zinnia, with a long narrow beak, swept wings, and a clear ruby throat patch. Fill the frame with the wings and flower so the small species never feels visually minor.

### Bramble — Uncommon

A saturated red northern cardinal with a pointed crest, black face mask, orange-red seed beak, and berry vine. Use the natural silhouette rather than a crown or costume.

### Fern — Uncommon

A warm brown Carolina wren with a long white eyebrow, fine wing bars, slender curved beak, and tail held upright beside a curled fern. The compact body and lifted tail should remain clear at 96 pixels.

### Tempo — Rare

A red-bellied woodpecker with a red crown and nape, pale buff face and breast, subtle rosy belly, bold black-and-white barred back, pointed bill, gripping feet, and stiff braced tail. Use a vertical garden post or trunk and rhythmic energy without written notation.

### Prism — Rare

A blue-gray belted kingfisher with a shaggy crest, long straight beak, white collar, blue chest band, and broad pond ripples catching restrained rainbow light. Keep the water secondary to the bird.

### Solstice — Legendary

A brilliant painted bunting with blue head, green back, red breast, and golden highlights in a sunlit garden arch. Rarity comes from authentic plumage, light, and composition rather than royal clothing or excessive sparkles.

## Sticker production guardrails

Sticker portraits should use the established polished storybook treatment: one large companion, readable face, crisp silhouette, convincing feather texture, gentle dimensionality, and a restrained garden setting. Preserve the real species' defining marks while allowing warm, expressive poses. No species should receive generic identical wings, beaks, or face geometry.

Vary the collection's compositions as well as its species: alternate perching with hovering, hopping, ground-level foraging, climbing, singing, and waterside action. Avoid repeating the same diagonal branch and resting pose across portraits.

Generate and review Sticker candidates one companion at a time. Keep every candidate source versioned, compare the whole image rather than only the requested revision, and integrate only approved portraits. Once all ten are accepted, optimize them to the standard 768-pixel production WebP and advance the catalog in one coherent content release.

## Acceptance checklist

- All ten birds remain distinguishable in the full-color and locked Classic contact sheets.
- The requested hummingbird, cardinal, goldfinch, and blue jay read immediately at phone size.
- Commons feel as desirable as higher rarities.
- Beaks, crests, tails, wings, feet, and defining markings do not drift between art styles.
- Classic portraits remain substantial at 96 pixels and do not disappear into scenery.
- Sticker preference falls back to Classic without broken images or ownership changes.
- Themes retain readable text contrast and motifs remain decorative rather than distracting.
- Catalog validation, dialogue validation, unit tests, production build, and phone/tablet visual checks pass.
